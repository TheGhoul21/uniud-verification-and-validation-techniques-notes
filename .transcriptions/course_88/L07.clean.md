Yesterday we started the closure under complementation of Büchi automata, which is a more involved topic. We introduced some basic concepts and proved a simple preliminary result. Today we will prove a couple of additional lemmas. One of them has a long and elegant proof that demonstrates the power of infinite-dimensional computations.

We previously introduced two fundamental concepts: congruence and saturation. A congruence is an equivalence relation that is compatible with concatenation. If x is equivalent to y and x-prime is equivalent to y-prime, then the concatenation xx-prime is equivalent to yy-prime. An example is the parity relation on the set of finite words, where words are partitioned into even-length and odd-length classes. This relation is a congruence because the parity of a concatenated word depends only on the parities of the components.

Saturation is an additional condition on congruences. Let U and V be equivalence classes of a congruence. If the intersection of U concatenated with V to the omega and a language L is non-empty (meaning there is at least one word in L of this form), then the entire set U concatenated with V to the omega must be included in L. If this holds, we say that the congruence saturates the language. The first result we proved is that if a relation saturates L, it also saturates the complement of L. This is important because we want to characterize the complement language.

We now introduce a new relation, denoted by double tilde A, which is built from a Büchi automaton A. This is a generalization of the relation tilde A that we used in the Myhill-Nerode theorem. Recall that tilde A relates two words if they lead from the initial state to the same state. This is useful for finite-state automata where acceptance is checked at the end of the scan. In the infinite setting, we cannot wait for the end of the scan, so we must generalize this relation.

We generalize the notation s arrow s-prime on w, which indicates that we can transition from state s to state s-prime by reading the finite word w. The set of all words that lead from s to s-prime is a regular language of finite words.

To adapt this to Büchi automata, we write s arrow s-prime on w with a superscript F to denote that we can go from s to s-prime by reading w while passing through at least one final state. This intermediate final state can possibly be s or s-prime.

As an exercise, you can prove that the language of all finite words w that lead from s to s-prime while encountering at least one final state is a regular language of finite words. The proof is similar to the standard reachability construction.

On the basis of this definition, we define the relation double tilde A associated with a Büchi automaton A. Let A equal Q, A, Delta, q0, F be a Büchi automaton. We define the relation double tilde A on the set of all finite words. Two words u and v belong to the same class of this relation if and only if, for all pairs of states s and s-prime in Q, the following two conditions hold. First, there is a path from s to s-prime reading u if and only if there is a path from s to s-prime reading v. Second, there is a path from s to s-prime reading u that visits a final state if and only if there is a path from s to s-prime reading v that visits a final state.

This relation puts two words in the same class if they behave identically with respect to reachability and the visiting of final states between any pair of states. This allows us to decompose an infinite computation into finite segments and compare computations on different words. We can ensure that if one computation visits a final state in a segment, the other computation does so as well. This relates directly to the Büchi acceptance condition.

For any Büchi automaton A, the relation double tilde A is a congruence of finite index that saturates L(A).

To prove this lemma, you should first show that double tilde A is an equivalence relation, that it is a congruence, and that it has a finite index. The finiteness of the index follows because the number of states is finite, so there are only finitely many pairs of states and finitely many reachability behaviors. We will focus on proving that double tilde A saturates L(A).

By our earlier corollary, if double tilde A saturates L(A), then it also saturates the complement of L(A). This is the key property we need for complementation.

To prove saturation, let U and V be equivalence classes of double tilde A. Let alpha be an omega-word belonging to the intersection of U concatenated with V to the omega and L(A). We assume this intersection is non-empty, and we want to show that the entire set U concatenated with V to the omega is included in L(A). This means that any other word beta in U concatenated with V to the omega must also belong to L(A).

Since alpha is in L(A), there exists a successful computation of A on alpha. Since alpha belongs to U concatenated with V to the omega, we can write alpha as the concatenation of a prefix u in U and an infinite sequence of segments v1, v2, and so on, in V.

We can identify the sequence of states visited at the boundaries of these segments in the successful computation of alpha. Let q0 be the initial state. Let s1 be the state reached after reading u. Let s2 be the state reached after reading v1 from s1. In general, let s-i-plus-1 be the state reached after reading vi from si. This gives us an infinite sequence of states q0, s1, s2, and so on. Since the computation is successful, it must visit a final state infinitely often. This means that for infinitely many segments vi, the transition from si to s-i-plus-1 visits at least one final state.

Now let beta be another word in U concatenated with V to the omega. We can write beta as the concatenation of a prefix u-prime in U and an infinite sequence of segments v1-prime, v2-prime, and so on, in V. We must show that beta is accepted by A.

By definition of double tilde A, since u and u-prime are in the same class U, they have the same reachability behavior. Since there is a path from q0 to s1 reading u, there is also a path from q0 to s1 reading u-prime. Similarly, since each vi-prime is in the same class V as vi, they share the same reachability properties. Thus, for each i, we can transition from si to s-i-plus-1 by reading vi-prime.

Furthermore, for the infinitely many segments where the transition from si to s-i-plus-1 under vi visits a final state, the transition under vi-prime must also visit a final state, by definition of the relation.

Therefore, we can construct a valid computation of A on beta that starts in the initial state q0, visits the sequence of states s1, s2, and so on, and passes through a final state infinitely often. Although the intermediate states within each segment may differ, the resulting computation is guaranteed to be successful. Thus, beta belongs to L(A). This completes the proof that double tilde A saturates L(A).

Consequently, double tilde A saturates the complement of L(A). This means that for any classes U and V of double tilde A, the set U concatenated with V to the omega is either completely contained in L(A) or completely contained in the complement of L(A).

We now prove a second lemma, which is a specific instance of Ramsey's theorem on omega-words.

Let tilde be a congruence of finite index on A star. For every omega-word alpha in A to the omega, there exist equivalence classes U and V of tilde such that alpha belongs to U concatenated with the vectorial closure of V, and V concatenated with V is a subset of V.

This theorem states that any omega-word can be characterized using these algebraic building blocks. To prove it, we define a merging relation on positions. Let alpha be an omega-word. We say that two positions k and k-prime merge at a position m in their future (where m > k and m > k-prime) if the segment alpha[k, m] is equivalent under tilde to the segment alpha[k-prime, m]. We write this as k equivalent to k-prime at m.

If two positions merge at m, they remain merged for all future positions m-prime > m. This follows from the right-invariance of the congruence: we can concatenate the suffix alpha[m, m-prime] to both equivalent segments, and the resulting segments alpha[k, m-prime] and alpha[k-prime, m-prime] will remain equivalent under tilde.

Since they remain merged forever, we can omit the parameter m and simply write that k and k-prime merge. This relation on positions is an equivalence relation of finite index. The finiteness of the index follows because tilde has a finite index.

Since this relation partitions the infinite set of natural numbers into a finite number of classes, there must exist at least one class that contains infinitely many positions. Let k0, k1, k2, and so on, be an infinite sequence of positions in this class.

Since tilde has a finite index, we can find an infinite subsequence of these positions such that the segments alpha[k0, ki] all belong to the same tilde class V for all i >= 1. Without loss of generality, we can assume that k0 > 0.

By construction, the prefix alpha[0, k0] belongs to some class U, and we have infinitely many positions k in the subsequence such that the segment alpha[k0, k] belongs to V. This means that the suffix of alpha starting at k0 has infinitely many prefixes in V. Therefore, alpha belongs to U concatenated with the vectorial closure of V.

We have characterized alpha using a prefix in U and a suffix in the vectorial closure of V. The next step is to relate this to the omega-closure notation U concatenated with V to the omega, which we will complete next week.
